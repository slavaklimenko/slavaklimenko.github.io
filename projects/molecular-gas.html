Research project 01

Molecular gas in the early Universe

H2, HD, CO, and neutral-carbon absorption in damped Lyα systems at z ≈ 2–3.

Research overview

Molecular absorbers reveal cold, dense gas in the interstellar medium of distant galaxies. Compared with local ISM gas, they show distinct abundance patterns, reflecting differences in the physical conditions between the Milky Way and galaxies at high redshift. These absorbers are rare, appearing in fewer than ∼0.5% of quasar spectra. We conducted a systematic search for H2 absorption systems in low-resolution quasar spectra from the SDSS and doubled the number of known high-redshift molecular absorbers at that time.

Cold molecular gas is the raw material for star formation, yet in distant galaxies it is extremely difficult to observe in emission. Damped Lyα systems seen in the spectra of background quasars give direct access to this gas through H2, HD, CO, and C I absorption lines.

Modelling the excitation of these species constrains the gas temperature, density, ultraviolet radiation field, and chemical enrichment — and, through CO, the temperature of the cosmic microwave background at the redshift of the absorber.

Observational approach

High-resolution optical spectroscopy of quasars with large ground-based telescopes resolves individual rotational levels of H2 and CO and the fine-structure levels of C I. Ultra-high-resolution VLT/ESPRESSO data additionally allow measurements of isotopic abundances and tests of fundamental cosmology at cosmic noon.

Measurements

  • Molecular hydrogen and HD absorption
  • Carbon monoxide absorption and excitation
  • Neutral-carbon fine-structure excitation
  • Gas temperature, density, and UV radiation field
  • CMB temperature at z ≈ 1.7–3
