Gas, dust & galaxy evolution

Viacheslav Klimenko

Postdoctoral Fellow, Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of South Carolina

I am an observational astronomer interested in the physical and chemical evolution of gas and dust in the interstellar and circumgalactic media of nearby and distant galaxies. My research focuses on understanding how gas and dust properties vary among different galaxy populations, with location within and around galaxies, and across cosmic time.

The central question is whether our understanding of the gas and dust physics in the local ISM can be broadly applied to studies of the ISM in other galaxies. Beyond the fundamental question of the Milky Way’s place among galaxies, answering this question has implications for extragalactic astrophysics and cosmology.

My research combines quasar and stellar absorption spectroscopy and reddening with galaxy imaging and integral-field spectroscopy. This lets me tie precise measurements of gas and dust along the sightlines to the properties of the distant galaxies that host them.

I work with ultraviolet, optical, infrared, and radio data from space telescopes — FUSE, HST, Spitzer, and JWST — and from large ground-based observatories, including Keck (Hawaii), the VLT (Chile), and ALMA (Chile).

I received my Ph.D. from the Ioffe Institute in St. Petersburg, where I worked as a Research Scientist from 2016 to 2022 and taught as an Assistant Professor at St. Petersburg Academic University. I have been at the University of South Carolina since 2022.

Research interests

All research
01

Molecular gas in the early Universe

H2, CO, and neutral carbon in damped Lyα systems at z ≈ 2–3, and what their excitation reveals about cold gas and the CMB.

 02

The baryon cycle around galaxies

Cold circumgalactic gas around nearby galaxies, mapped with HST/COS absorption and SDSS/MaNGA integral-field spectroscopy.

 03

Dust in distant galaxies

Extinction curves, the 2175 Å bump, and silicate and ice features measured with JWST and ground-based spectroscopy.
